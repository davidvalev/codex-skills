# Theme briefing

## Intake gate

The brand name, canonical domain, and theme prompt are mandatory. Ask for missing mandatory inputs. Do not rename, respell, or shorten the supplied brand without approval.

The theme prompt can be short. Decide whether a theme interview would materially improve the output. Skip questions already answered by the user.

## Screening questions

Ask one compact group of no more than six questions. Choose only the questions that change creative decisions:

- Who is the primary user, and what do they hope to get from a conversation?
- What role should the chat play, such as coach, guide, critic, tutor, companion, or specialist?
- Which three to five conversation topics matter most?
- What should the brand feel like, and what should it never feel like?
- Should the visuals lean toward photography, illustration, abstract 3D, or a restrained graphic system?
- Which language and market should the copy address?
- Are there topics, claims, symbols, colors, or visual subjects to avoid?
- Are there reference brands the user likes or dislikes, and what specifically should be learned from them?
- For a consequential theme, where should the chat stop and direct users to a qualified person or emergency resource?

Do not ask about token quantities, package prices, price-per-message, discounts, free tokens, currency, token expiry, legal entity details, or policy jurisdiction. These are outside this skill.

## Working from incomplete answers

If the user does not answer optional questions, continue with reasonable creative decisions. Record them under `Creative decisions` in `assumptions.md`. Do not present invented choices as user facts.

## Canonical brief

Before writing copy or creating art, establish:

- brand name and exact capitalization
- canonical domain and URL form
- theme in one sentence
- primary audience
- chat role
- five core conversation jobs
- desired and prohibited tone
- visual medium and mood
- language and locale
- safety boundary, when applicable
- words, claims, and motifs to avoid

Use this brief as the single source for every deliverable.
